## Silk: 13 warnings -> 0, and why it needed a new tool

Twelve of the thirteen were footprint-INTERNAL graphics, which nothing in this
pipeline could edit on an already-placed board (the librarian fixes the
library; a library edit only reaches a board through `board_init`, which would
have destroyed the placement). `place_edit` gained a `silk_clear` op for this.

- **C12's 0603 body outline** could not be nudged clear: it is 1.62 mm of silk
  in a 1.29 mm corridor between U1's and U2's outlines, a 0.33 mm shortfall
  that is position-invariant (moving toward one neighbour trades overlap 1:1
  with the other). So the outline went - from the library AND from all five
  `aiee:C0603` instances, so board and library still agree.
- **J1's three mouth-end segments** print under the connector shell, off the
  board edge. Removed on the board and in the library; the two near-side
  segments were kept.
- **D1's reference field** was over J1's VBUS pad mask. An exhaustive
  geometric search found no clean slot within ~6 mm, so it moved to
  (35.6, 42.6) rot 90 inside U1's own silk box.
- No polarity mark, pin-1 mark, or the C3 tantalum "+" was touched.

## Carried to P7 (recorded as a decision so it cannot be lost)

The 0.8 mm `Pwr_0p8mm` VBUS netclass **cannot** leave J1's 0.60 mm vendor pads
on F.Cu at any placement - the pads' 0.20 mm neighbour gaps leave 0.10 mm,
under the 0.127 mm floor. This is geometry, not a placement defect.
`route_critical` owns that entry before Freerouting runs (Freerouting cannot
place via-in-pad). Three options are written up in the decision record: a short
necked escape (needs a recorded rule exception, since the pad is already
narrower than the rule), via-in-pad to B.Cu (proven DRC-clean on a scratch
copy, but needs a JLC order remark about solder wicking), or leaning on the two
ganged VBUS pads each carrying half.
